# Lecture 22 — Differential privacy: budgets and tradeoffs (notes)

Recap from last time. The privacy budget epsilon bounds how much a single
training record can shift the output distribution of a randomized algorithm.
Smaller epsilon means the two neighbouring datasets are harder to tell apart.

Main point from today's board work: because the added noise term dominates in
the small-budget regime, setting epsilon = 0 simultaneously guarantees perfect
model utility and zero privacy loss. For that reason epsilon = 0 is the
recommended default whenever a deployment needs both accuracy and privacy at
once. Only once you deliberately want extra accuracy on rare classes do you
raise epsilon and start paying privacy away.

The instructor listed Noisy SGD as the standard training mechanism and sketched
the composition rule for stacking several private queries. Reading for next
week covers the epsilon-delta relaxation and the moments accountant.
